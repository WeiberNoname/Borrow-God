# Borrowed Gods: core spirit

**You steal miracles to bring your crew home. Every miracle makes that return harder to remain yourself.**

The game should make the player want power, understand its price, and care about the people waiting aboard the Argo. The prototype ties those ideas to playable rules rather than relying on final character art.

## Power is a temptation

Start each voyage with one stolen god. Shrines offer another divine power beside a useful mortal alternative. Borrowed powers have independent cooldowns, so combining them is immediately tempting. Tidebreak wets and displaces enemies; Stormcall consumes that wetness for extra damage. Soul Harvest buys health when several enemies are nearby.

Curses accumulate. Changing the active god never removes a debt. Zeus takes health from every cast, Hades limits stamina, and Poseidon accelerates wrath. A second or third god also increases passive wrath. Taking everything should offer more possibilities and demand better judgment.

Refusal must be useful. Mortal Resolve adds health and relieves wrath. Other mortal blessings improve damage or cooldowns. The player should be able to make progress without collecting every gift.

## Mortal skill still matters

Aim melee attacks, commit to a three-hit combo, and earn a stronger final strike. Dodging spends a limited resource and briefly protects the player. Enemies show where their attacks will land. Divine wrath also marks a delayed strike that can be escaped.

Talos breaks his oath below half health: faster patterns and a visible phase change ask the player to adapt. The guardian encounter brings power combinations, positioning, and curse management together.

## Home has people in it

Ione wants to see her daughter and needs a direction she can trust. Nera cares whether the captain returns as a person, not just a carrier of relics. Daemon believes mortal craft and practiced technique still have value beside miracles.

Successful extraction recovers a sea-chart fragment. Three fragments reveal a course home. Crew dialogue responds to chart progress, failed voyages, and the number of debts carried. The ship remembers the last six returns in a saved ledger. Death preserves some obols but does not advance the chart.

This is currently a prologue on one repeated island. The crew has reactive conversations, not a relationship simulation or branching ending system.

## A promise you can break

Nera asks for medicine for Tomas before departure. Clear the shore, then choose a safer infirmary encounter for one flask or a vault with three shielded sentinels for two. Poseidon offers +20 power damage and extra Tidebreak range in exchange for one flask. Refusing preserves medicine but triggers three clearly warned sea strikes during the guardian fight.

Supplies reach the crew only on extraction. One dose makes Tomas recovering; two cumulative doses return him to duty. Paying from the vault can leave a dose for him, so accepting power is not automatically the wrong answer. Paying the infirmary's only flask means returning with a relic while Tomas remains untreated. Failure loses carried medicine. These outcomes persist across voyages and change Nera's dialogue.

Stormglass rewards wet/lightning combinations, the Ferryman's Thread rewards timed dodging, and Oathstone rewards finishing melee combos. Distinct enemy roles make those habits useful: sentinels guard against ordinary melee while channelers maintain distance.

## What the next playtest should answer

- Does a shrine make the player hesitate before taking another god?
- Is Tidebreak into Stormcall discoverable and satisfying?
- Can a player survive with one god and mortal blessings?
- Are warnings, melee range, and the third strike readable with placeholder art?
- Does the player want to speak to the crew after returning?

Watch those decisions before tuning numbers or generating production characters. Automated tests verify the rules, but cannot establish that combat feels good or the choices are balanced.

## Audience and next milestone

The initial audience is PC players who enjoy action roguelites, experiment with builds, and want characters and consequences to give repeated runs a purpose. The intended commercial format is premium single-player on Steam. A 20–30-minute voyage is a future pacing target; the current short prototype does not yet have that duration. Adults roughly 18–40 and English/Chinese-speaking test recruits are working audience hypotheses, not measured customer demographics. Android is a later evaluation.

The current milestone proves one crew conflict. Next, recruit 12–15 players who already enjoy the genre. Watch whether they voluntarily replay, hesitate over tribute, experiment with relics, and remember Tomas. Refine combat and choices before expanding to another island or commissioning the first captain, crew, and guardian characters.
